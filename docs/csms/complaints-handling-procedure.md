# CSMS Complaints Handling Procedure

**Status:** Internal working draft — not approved for customer use.  
**Proposed reference:** TNCV-CSMS-PRO-CMP-001  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Owner:** [Management to assign]  
**Approval authority:** [Management to assign]

## 1. Purpose

Establish an accessible, impartial, confidential and traceable process for receiving, assessing, investigating, deciding, responding to and learning from complaints concerning the proposed CSMS Readiness Review & Documentation Support service.

The procedure is designed to handle dissatisfaction about TNCVCertification's own service, conduct, process, communications, confidentiality or deliverables. It does not create certification, accreditation, principal qualification, vendor approval or appeal authority.

## 2. Scope and definitions

A **complaint** is an expression of dissatisfaction by a client or other interested party about TNCVCertification's service, personnel, process, communication, handling of information or issued deliverable, where a response or resolution is explicitly or reasonably expected.

Examples include:
- Alleged poor service, delay, discourtesy, misleading communication or failure to follow agreed scope.
- Alleged error, inconsistency, unfair treatment or failure to follow the approved procedure.
- Alleged confidentiality, privacy, information-security or evidence-handling failure.
- Concern about impartiality, conflict of interest, pressure, retaliation or staff conduct.
- Disagreement with how TNCVCertification handled a factual correction or other service process.

This process distinguishes:
- **Client factual correction (RA-09):** a request to correct factual content in a draft report, handled under the [Client Factual Review & Correction Procedure](client-factual-review-procedure.md). If the concern is also about unfair treatment or process misconduct, register a complaint as well and cross-reference both records.
- **Technical disagreement:** a challenge to assessment interpretation, evidence sufficiency, methodology or technical reasoning. Route for independent technical review under the approved technical review procedure; register a complaint if there is also alleged service failure, misconduct or procedural unfairness.
- **Principal/client decision dispute:** a challenge to a principal's score, vendor status, tender, qualification or procurement decision. Direct the complainant to the relevant principal/client; do not represent TNCVCertification as an appeal authority. A complaint about TNCVCertification's own conduct in communicating or supporting that process remains in scope.
- **Incident report:** a suspected privacy/security or safety incident must also be escalated immediately through the relevant incident process. A complaint record does not replace incident response.

## 3. Principles and safeguards

1. **Accessibility:** accept complaints through management-approved channels and do not impose unnecessary format requirements.
2. **No retaliation:** a complainant must not be threatened, disadvantaged or treated unfairly for raising a concern in good faith.
3. **Impartiality:** screen conflicts before assignment. The person whose work or decision is challenged must not control investigation, findings or approval of the response.
4. **Independence:** appoint an investigator who was not responsible for the subject matter. If internal separation is not practicable, appoint a competent external or otherwise independent investigator.
5. **Fairness:** define allegations clearly, examine relevant evidence objectively, and give people whose conduct is questioned a reasonable opportunity to respond without giving them control over the outcome.
6. **Confidentiality and data minimization:** restrict information to those who need it; use only approved channels and systems; collect only information needed to investigate and resolve the complaint.
7. **Timeliness:** acknowledge, update and respond within targets formally approved by management. No response period is created by this draft.
8. **Traceability:** preserve original complaint wording, evidence references, decisions, communications and changes.
9. **Separation of remedy and technical judgement:** do not alter a technically supported finding merely to appease a complainant. Correct substantiated errors through controlled technical/report revision.
10. **Learning:** assess trends, root causes, corrective action and effectiveness without assuming every complaint is justified or every complaint is evidence of a systemic failure.

## 4. Roles and decision authority

| Role | Responsibility | Required independence / evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Complaint recipient / coordinator | Receive, record, screen completeness, issue acknowledgement, safeguard records and monitor due dates | Trained and appointed; not permitted to decide own complaint |
| Complaint investigator | Define issues, gather and evaluate evidence, interview relevant parties and prepare findings | Competent and independent of challenged activity/person; conflict check recorded |
| Independent complaint decision reviewer | Review investigation sufficiency, findings, proposed outcome and response before issue | Not the investigator or subject of complaint where practicable; delegated authority |
| Management escalation authority | Decide serious, high-risk, unresolved or conflict-related matters and approve resources/remedies | Documented authority; independent of commercial pressure and subject of complaint |
| Corrective-action owner | Implement approved correction/corrective action | Must not self-verify effectiveness |
| Effectiveness verifier | Verify action implementation and effectiveness | Competent and independent from action implementation |
| Document controller | Maintain restricted case records, access, retention, legal hold and revision history | Approved access and retention arrangements |

The investigator must not be the person whose actions are complained about. The subject of a complaint may provide a factual response and evidence but must not select the investigator, determine the findings, approve the response or suppress records.

Where a complaint concerns the complaint coordinator, investigator, decision reviewer or senior management, escalate to an alternative independent authority or external investigator. If effective independence cannot be established, disclose the limitation, pause the affected decision and obtain an independent route; do not claim impartial investigation.

## 5. Receipt and registration

Complaints may be received in writing or verbally through a channel formally approved and published by management. A verbal complaint must be documented by the recipient and, where practicable, confirmed with the complainant. Anonymous complaints may be recorded and assessed to the extent that available information permits; inability to contact the complainant must not automatically prevent an appropriate internal review.

At intake:
1. Assign a unique complaint ID and record date/time received, channel and receiving person.
2. Preserve the complainant's original wording or the closest accurate record of a verbal complaint; distinguish quotation from recipient summary.
3. Record complainant identity/contact only where provided and necessary; note any confidentiality or safe-contact request.
4. Identify the service, engagement, report, personnel, dates and events concerned.
5. Record the issue, expected outcome, urgency, potential harm and any immediate containment request.
6. Identify supporting documents, witnesses, communications and controlled evidence references. Do not place sensitive attachments in an unrestricted register.
7. Acknowledge receipt within the approved acknowledgement target, using the requested safe channel where feasible; record the acknowledgement or reason it could not be sent.
8. Explain the process, next update point, confidentiality limits and that response targets are subject to approved policy and case complexity.

Do not reject a complaint merely because it is inconvenient, critical, anonymous or not submitted on a preferred form. If it is outside scope, explain the reason and route it to the appropriate body where known.

## 6. Triage, risk and conflict screening

The coordinator records:
- Whether the matter concerns TNCVCertification or an external principal/client decision.
- Whether it is a factual correction, technical disagreement, complaint, incident report or a combination.
- Alleged impact, urgency, continuing harm, safety/security/privacy concern and risk of evidence loss.
- Whether work, report release, access, communication or data processing should be temporarily paused pending review.
- Conflict checks for the coordinator, investigator, decision reviewer and management escalation authority.
- Whether legal, privacy, security, impartiality, safeguarding or external specialist advice is required.

Immediately route suspected data/security incidents to the [Data Incident Response Playbook](data-incident-response-playbook.md), and preserve relevant records. Complaint handling must not delay urgent containment or any legally required response.

Potential serious misconduct, fraud/falsification, retaliation, threats, confidentiality breach, conflict concealment or management involvement must be escalated to the independent management authority or external route. Record the triage rationale and interim safeguards.

## 7. Acknowledgement and communication targets

Management must approve and publish:
- Acknowledgement target and method.
- Initial triage target.
- Target for investigation plan and first progress update.
- Periodic update frequency for open cases.
- Target for final response and escalation when it cannot be met.
- Authority for extending a target and required notice to the complainant.

These targets must be realistic for the organization's resources and any applicable legal or contractual obligations. They must be stated in the approved policy/service information and monitored. **This draft intentionally does not invent numerical deadlines.**

If a target is at risk, the coordinator records the reason, informs the complainant where contact is possible, provides the next update date and obtains any required extension approval. Silence is not closure.

## 8. Investigation

The appointed investigator prepares an investigation plan proportionate to the allegations, including:
1. Allegation(s), scope, questions to be answered and applicable contract/procedure/criteria.
2. Investigator appointment, competence and conflict/independence decision.
3. Records to preserve and obtain, including original communications, report versions, source criteria, evidence logs, approvals and relevant system records.
4. People to interview and method for recording statements.
5. Need for technical, legal, privacy, security or external specialist input.
6. Risks, interim safeguards, access restrictions and target dates.

The investigator must:
- Preserve original records and chain of custody where relevant; do not alter or backdate evidence.
- Examine evidence supporting and contradicting each allegation.
- Record facts separately from inference, opinion and unverified statements.
- Give relevant personnel a fair opportunity to respond, subject to confidentiality, safety and investigation integrity.
- Record interviews, document review, gaps, limitations and corroboration.
- Avoid unnecessary disclosure of complainant or third-party identity.
- Identify whether the issue is isolated, recurring, systemic or not substantiated.
- Refer technical matters to a competent independent technical authority; the investigator must not exceed their competence.
- Record when evidence is unavailable and how that limits the conclusion.

## 9. Findings, decision and response

The investigator prepares a report containing:
- Complaint ID and defined allegations.
- Investigation scope, methods, evidence references and limitations.
- Findings for each allegation: substantiated, partly substantiated, not substantiated, unable to determine, or outside scope.
- Evidence-based rationale and any procedural or control failure identified.
- Proposed correction, corrective action, remedy or referral.
- Any residual risk and follow-up verification needed.

The independent complaint decision reviewer checks investigation independence, completeness, fairness, evidence sufficiency, rationale, proportionality and proposed response. The reviewer records approval, rejection or required further work. The subject of the complaint cannot approve the decision or response.

The response should, as appropriate and lawful:
- Confirm the complaint issue(s) considered and investigation scope.
- State the outcome for each allegation and a concise rationale.
- Explain correction, remedy or corrective action taken/planned without disclosing restricted personnel or third-party information.
- Explain any issue outside TNCVCertification's authority and the route to the relevant principal/client or other body.
- Identify a further contact or escalation route, if approved.
- Avoid unsupported admissions, defensive language, disclosure of confidential records or claims that exceed the evidence.

If the complaint is not substantiated, respond respectfully and explain the evidence basis. If it is partly substantiated or substantiated, acknowledge the established service failure and communicate proportionate remedy and corrective action. Do not promise an outcome outside delegated authority.

## 10. Correction, corrective action and effectiveness

Distinguish:
- **Correction / immediate remedy:** addresses the specific error or impact (for example, correcting an inaccurate communication or replacing a report through controlled revision).
- **Corrective action:** addresses the cause to prevent recurrence (for example, procedure change, training, supervision, system control or role separation).

For each action, record the issue/root cause, action, owner, approved due date, resources, implementation evidence, residual risk and effectiveness criteria. A completed action is not effective merely because it was marked done.

An independent verifier checks:
1. The action was implemented as approved.
2. Evidence supports the claimed change.
3. The original cause or contributing factor has been addressed.
4. A proportionate sample, test, monitoring period or subsequent case supports effectiveness.
5. Any recurrence or unintended consequence is recorded and escalated.

If effectiveness fails, reopen or create a linked action and escalate to management. Do not allow the action owner to be the sole effectiveness verifier.

## 11. Closure, reopening and external escalation

A complaint may be administratively closed only when:
- Investigation findings and decision are approved by the delegated independent reviewer.
- The complainant has been sent the approved response, or inability to contact them is documented.
- Immediate remedies and required corrective actions have owners and approved due dates.
- Any action not yet complete remains tracked as an open linked action; complaint closure must not conceal overdue corrective action.
- Required notifications, incident records, legal holds and cross-references are complete.
- The case record is complete, access restricted and closure date/authority recorded.

Reopen or escalate a case if material new evidence appears, the response was not implemented, corrective action fails, retaliation is alleged, a similar complaint recurs or a significant control failure is discovered.

Where an external route is appropriate, record referral, consent/authority where required, information disclosed, date and recipient. Do not imply that an external principal or regulator has accepted or resolved the complaint unless evidence exists.

## 12. Monitoring, management review and learning

At a management-approved interval, review aggregated and appropriately anonymized information:
- Number and type of complaints, sources and affected services.
- Acknowledgement and response timeliness against approved targets.
- Outcomes, recurring themes, severity and root causes.
- Corrective-action status, overdue actions and effectiveness failures.
- Reopened cases, escalations and any retaliation/conflict concerns.
- Resource, competence, procedure or communication improvements.

Do not use low complaint counts alone as proof of service quality; assess accessibility and confidence in the reporting route. Management review decisions, owners and due dates must be recorded.

## 13. Records, privacy and retention

Maintain, as applicable:
- Original complaint and intake record.
- Acknowledgement and all relevant communications.
- Triage, conflict checks and interim safeguards.
- Investigator appointment and investigation plan.
- Evidence index, interview records and findings.
- Independent decision review and approved response.
- Correction/corrective-action records and effectiveness verification.
- External referral, incident cross-reference, reopening and closure evidence.
- Trend analysis and management review.

Store complaint records in an approved restricted system and channel under the approved [Data Governance & Information Security Procedure](data-governance-information-security-procedure.md). Apply access limitation, confidentiality, legal hold, retention and secure disposal requirements. Complaint records may contain sensitive allegations and personal data; do not collect or circulate more than necessary. If approved privacy/security controls and storage are not available, do not invite or receive sensitive complaint attachments through an unapproved route; escalate and provide a safe approved alternative once established.

## 14. Approval and operational-readiness gate

Before this procedure is used with customers, management must approve:
- Complaint scope, policy, accessible contact route and public-facing instructions.
- Acknowledgement, progress-update, final-response and extension targets.
- Complaint coordinator, investigator, independent decision reviewer and escalation authority, including external fallback.
- Competence, independence, conflict-screening and no-retaliation safeguards.
- Decision authority, remedies, corrective-action and effectiveness verification.
- Privacy/security channels, restricted records access, retention and legal-hold controls.
- Training, resources, monitoring and management-review arrangements.

This draft does not appoint personnel, approve response deadlines, create a live complaint channel or demonstrate operational readiness. Until the gates are approved and verified, it is an internal working document only.
