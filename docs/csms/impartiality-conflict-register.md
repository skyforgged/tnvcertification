# CSMS Impartiality & Conflict of Interest Register

**Status:** Internal working framework — requires management approval and engagement-level implementation.  
**Reference:** TNCV-CSMS-IMP-001 (proposed)  
**Version:** 0.2 — RA-06 remediation draft  
**Owner:** [Management to appoint an impartiality / compliance function]

## 1. Purpose, principles and boundaries

Identify, evaluate, record, control and monitor actual, potential and perceived threats to impartiality in the proposed CSMS Readiness Review & Documentation Support service.

Decisions and findings must be based on objective evidence and must not be influenced by commercial, personal, financial, organizational or other pressures. Personnel must disclose relevant relationships and must not decide for themselves that a disclosed conflict is harmless.

This service framework does not create authority to certify, qualify, approve, score or register contractors on behalf of a principal, regulator or scheme owner. Client disclosure or consent does not by itself eliminate a conflict or make an otherwise unacceptable arrangement acceptable.

## 2. Definitions

- **Actual conflict:** a current relationship or interest that compromises, or directly interferes with, objective performance.
- **Potential conflict:** a circumstance that could develop into an actual conflict during the engagement.
- **Perceived conflict:** a circumstance that a reasonable informed stakeholder could view as impairing objectivity, even if personnel believe they can remain objective.
- **Threat:** a condition or influence that could impair impartiality.
- **Safeguard:** a specific measure intended to eliminate a threat or reduce it to an acceptable level, supported by evidence of implementation and effectiveness.
- **Independent reviewer:** a person with relevant competence who did not create the work under review and has no unresolved conflict affecting the review.

## 3. Mandatory screening points

Screen and record conflicts:
1. At initial enquiry and before proposal / contract acceptance.
2. Before assigning each person, subcontractor or external expert.
3. Before work starts and before any site visit or interview.
4. When client, principal, scope, personnel, ownership or commercial arrangements change.
5. Before technical review and report approval.
6. When a complaint, allegation, external pressure or new relationship is disclosed.
7. Before continuing work after a material change or incident.

A new conflict disclosure triggers reassessment. Pause the affected activity where continued work could compromise objectivity.

## 4. Threat categories and screening prompts

| Category | Screening prompts / examples | Possible control |
|---|---|---|
| Self-review | Did TNCV or assigned personnel prepare, implement, train on, or materially revise the same system/evidence now being assessed? | Separate personnel and workstreams; independent reviewer; narrow scope or decline |
| Advocacy | Has anyone represented the contractor in a tender, dispute, principal submission or approval campaign? | Remove advocacy role; restrict scope; decline if objectivity cannot be restored |
| Familiarity / relationship | Family, close personal, employment, former employment, board, partnership or repeated close working relationship? | Reassign; independent review; document residual risk |
| Financial / ownership | Direct or indirect ownership, investment, debt, gifts, benefits or other financial interest? | Remove interest where feasible; reassign; decline if material |
| Commercial pressure | Success fee, contingent payment, referral commission, target bonus or payment linked to pass, score, approval or tender outcome? | Prohibit outcome-linked incentives; revise fee terms; escalate |
| Intimidation / undue influence | Pressure from client, principal, sales, management or another party to suppress, alter or delay findings? | Escalate, preserve records, independent decision, pause or terminate |
| Dependency | Excessive revenue concentration, repeated engagements or dependence on one client / referral source? | Management-level evaluation, independent oversight, capacity and revenue safeguards |
| Confidentiality / information | Access to competitor, principal or third-party confidential information; incompatible duties or information barriers? | Access restriction, segregation, secure handling, decline if duties conflict |
| Subcontractor / external expert | Relationships, prior services, financial interest or competing engagements of external resources? | Equivalent screening, contractual controls, replacement |
| Role incompatibility | Same person prepares evidence and independently evaluates it, reviews own work, or approves own report? | Segregate roles; independent competent review; do not waive hard stops |

## 5. Risk evaluation and decision rules

Evaluate the facts, likelihood, potential impact on objectivity, affected decisions, duration, people involved and effectiveness of available safeguards. Do not use a numeric average that masks a severe threat.

| Level | Decision guide | Minimum response |
|---|---|---|
| Low | No material threat identified after screening | Record rationale, approver and monitoring trigger |
| Medium | Threat is identifiable and can be reduced through practical safeguards | Document safeguards, owner, evidence, residual risk and independent approval before work |
| High | Material threat; safeguards uncertain or significant role separation needed | Pause acceptance/work; escalate to independent management review; reassign, narrow scope or decline |
| Unacceptable | Objectivity cannot be protected, a hard-stop condition exists, or improper influence is demanded | Decline or stop affected engagement; do not trade off against revenue or client consent |

### Hard-stop conditions

Do not accept or continue the affected work where:
- A material self-review conflict cannot be removed through genuine separation and independent review.
- A person is required to assess or approve their own advisory / implementation work as the sole independent reviewer.
- Payment, commission or reward is conditional on a passing result, approval, score or tender outcome and cannot be removed.
- A material financial, personal or organizational interest cannot be eliminated or controlled.
- Client or other party demands suppression, falsification or improper alteration of evidence/findings.
- Confidentiality or legal obligations make objective and lawful performance impossible.
- No competent and sufficiently independent reviewer/decision authority can be provided for a necessary control.

Disclosure, client consent, confidentiality undertakings or an extra signature alone do not cure a hard-stop conflict.

## 7. Safeguard design and effectiveness

Safeguards must be tailored to the identified threat. Examples include:
- Reassign the affected person and verify the replacement's competence and conflict status.
- Separate advisory/document-support work from assessment, technical review and report approval.
- Appoint a competent independent reviewer who did not prepare or materially change the work.
- Remove success-based compensation, referral commission or outcome-linked incentives.
- Restrict access to confidential information and implement tested information barriers.
- Narrow the service to a clearly described non-evaluative activity, where this genuinely removes the threat and is transparently communicated.
- Obtain independent management oversight and monitor defined indicators.
- Decline or terminate the engagement where controls cannot be effective.

For every safeguard record: threat addressed, action, accountable owner, due date, implementation evidence, effectiveness check, reviewer and result. A planned safeguard is not an implemented safeguard.

## 8. Role separation and independence

1. A person who prepared, implemented, trained on or materially revised a client's system/evidence must not independently conclude on the adequacy of that same work where self-review threatens objectivity.
2. An assessor must not be the sole technical reviewer or sole approver of their own report.
3. The impartiality reviewer must be sufficiently independent from sales, delivery and the engagement outcome, and have authority to restrict, pause, reject or terminate work.
4. The technical reviewer must be competent for the actual principal criteria, sector and activity and independent of the work reviewed.
5. Management must not override an unacceptable impartiality decision for commercial reasons. Any disagreement must be recorded and escalated to an independent authority; hard stops remain in force.
6. Where organizational size prevents full separation, document the threat and genuinely independent external safeguard. If no effective safeguard exists, decline the work.
7. Role combinations must be evaluated engagement by engagement; a general job title or policy statement is insufficient.

## 9. Monitoring, escalation and records

Personnel and external resources must disclose new or changed circumstances promptly. The service owner must notify the impartiality reviewer, assess impact on work already performed, and decide whether to pause, reassign, repeat review, correct a report or terminate the engagement.

Escalate attempts to influence findings, conceal conflicts, bypass safeguards or retaliate against personnel. Preserve relevant records and restrict access to those who need them.

Maintain declarations, evaluations, decisions, safeguard evidence, monitoring, communications and closure records in the approved controlled repository. Apply the approved privacy, access, retention and disposal rules; do not collect irrelevant personal data.

## 10. Engagement closure and periodic review

At engagement close, confirm:
- All disclosed conflicts and changes were evaluated.
- Safeguards were implemented and effectiveness checked.
- Any residual risk was accepted by an authorized independent role.
- Allegations, incidents and impartiality-related complaints were escalated and recorded.
- Lessons learned and required corrective actions were assigned.

Review the overall impartiality framework at the management review interval and after material incidents, complaints, changes to service model or recurring conflict patterns.
