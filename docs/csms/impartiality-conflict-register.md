## 1. Purpose

Identify, evaluate, record, and control threats to impartiality in the proposed CSMS Readiness Review & Documentation Support service.

The service must be delivered objectively. Commercial pressure, prior advisory work, personal relationships, financial interests, and organizational arrangements must not influence findings or reporting.

This register does not create authority to certify, qualify, or approve contractors on behalf of a principal.

## 2. Conflict screening triggers

Screen at enquiry, contract review, personnel assignment, scope change, report review, and whenever a new relationship or risk becomes known.

Consider:
- Prior consultancy, document preparation, training, or implementation support for the same contractor or scope.
- The same person advising on closure of a gap and later independently evaluating that work.
- Financial, ownership, employment, family, or close personal relationships.
- Referral commissions, contingent fees, success fees, or incentives linked to a passing result or tender award.
- Pressure from sales, management, clients, or principals to change findings.
- Repeated dependence on one client or other commercial relationships that could affect objectivity.
- Confidentiality obligations or access to competitor/client information.
- Use of subcontractors, external experts, or shared personnel.
- Any actual, potential, or perceived conflict identified by personnel or stakeholders.

## 3. Threat evaluation

| Level | Guide | Required response |
|---|---|---|
| Low | No material threat identified; routine controls sufficient | Record rationale and monitor |
| Medium | Threat can potentially be reduced through documented safeguards | Define owner, safeguards, independent review, and approval before work |
| High | Objectivity may be compromised; safeguards may not be sufficient | Reassign, separate service models, narrow scope, or decline |
| Unacceptable | Actual conflict cannot be effectively controlled or client demands improper conduct | Do not accept or stop the engagement |

This is an internal qualitative risk tool, not a standardized or externally accredited score.

## 5. Safeguard examples

Safeguards must address the specific threat and be demonstrably effective. Examples:
- Assign a different assessor who was not involved in advisory work.
- Separate advisory/document-support personnel from independent reviewers and decision roles.
- Require an independent technical review by a competent person with no relevant conflict.
- Remove success-based compensation or incentives tied to outcomes.
- Disclose relevant relationships to the client and obtain agreement where appropriate; disclosure alone does not cure a conflict.
- Restrict access to confidential information and apply information barriers.
- Narrow the scope to document organization or education rather than an independent readiness conclusion.
- Decline the engagement if impartiality cannot be protected.

A person who prepared or materially revised a client's evidence should not independently attest to the adequacy of that same work where this creates a conflict. The service owner must document the separation decision.

## 7. Escalation and ongoing monitoring

Personnel must promptly disclose a new or changed conflict. The service owner must reassess the engagement and may pause work, replace personnel, add independent review, revise scope, or terminate the engagement.

Any attempt to influence findings or suppress material observations must be escalated to the designated compliance/management function and recorded.

## 8. Complaints and allegations

Record impartiality-related complaints and allegations separately from ordinary service feedback. Assign an investigator who is independent of the work concerned where practicable. Document investigation, evidence, outcome, corrective action, and client communication.
