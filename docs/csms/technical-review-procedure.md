# CSMS Technical Review & Quality Assurance Procedure

**Document ID:** TNCV-CSMS-PRO-TR-001  
**Version:** 0.1 — Draft for management review  
**Status:** INTERNAL WORKING PROCEDURE — NOT APPROVED  
**Related remediation:** RA-07

> This procedure defines an internal technical quality review for CSMS readiness-assessment work products. It does not confer certification, accreditation, principal approval, contractor qualification, or regulatory authority.

## 1. Purpose

Ensure that assessment conclusions and reports are technically sound, evidence-based, traceable, within the agreed scope, and authorized before release. Technical review is a distinct quality-control activity performed after the assessor has completed the work product and before an authorized report approver releases it.

## 2. Scope

Apply to each CSMS readiness review deliverable, including the assessment plan, requirement/evidence mapping, sampling decisions, evidence evaluation, findings, limitations, client factual-comment dispositions, and final report. Apply again after any material correction, new evidence, scope change, or report revision that could affect conclusions.

This procedure governs TNCVCertification's readiness-review deliverables only. It does not replace a principal's or scheme owner's own evaluation, certification decision, vendor registration, statutory inspection, or regulatory authorization.

## 3. Terms and review outcomes

- **Technical reviewer:** competent person assigned to independently evaluate the technical quality of the work product.
- **Author / assessor:** person(s) who performed the assessment or prepared/materially changed the report.
- **Report approver:** person with documented delegated authority to authorize issue after required reviews.
- **Major issue:** error, omission, unsupported conclusion, scope breach, traceability failure, or unresolved conflict that may materially affect findings, conclusions, or intended use.
- **Minor issue:** localized correction that does not materially change evidence interpretation or conclusions.
- **Review outcome:** Accept; Accept subject to minor corrections verified; Return for major correction / rework; or Escalate unresolved technical disagreement.

No report may be released while a major issue, required review, or mandatory approval remains open.

## 4. Roles, competence and independence

1. Reviewer competence must be demonstrated for the specific sector, principal criteria, CSMS subject matter, assessment method, evidence evaluation, sampling, and report type being reviewed. Competence evidence may include verified qualifications/credentials, relevant experience, observed review tasks, assessed work samples, training with effectiveness evaluation, and current authorization.
2. Reviewer appointment and permitted scope must be documented in the personnel/authorization register before assignment. A generic job title or course attendance alone is insufficient.
3. The reviewer must not review their own assessment, evidence evaluation, finding, or report authorship. The reviewer must not have materially prepared the client's evidence or delivered advisory/implementation work that creates a self-review threat for the same scope.
4. The reviewer must complete an engagement-specific conflict declaration and be screened under the impartiality controls before access to the review assignment.
5. Where internal separation is not practicable, an independent external reviewer may be used only after competence, confidentiality, conflict, access, and delegation controls are verified. If independence or competence cannot be demonstrated, pause the review and do not release the report.
6. The report approver must be separately identified and hold documented release authority. The approver may rely on the technical review record but must not treat it as a substitute for their own release-gate confirmation.
7. No role in this procedure may claim authority to issue a principal's decision, certificate, accreditation, or regulatory approval unless that authority is separately evidenced by the relevant competent body.

## 5. Review inputs and readiness gate

The review package must include, as applicable:

- Approved engagement scope, criteria and current source references, including source verification and applicability rationale.
- Assessment plan, dates, locations, participants and methods.
- Requirement/evidence matrix with unique IDs and traceability.
- Evidence register with source, version, status, examination notes and limitations.
- Sampling population, rationale, selection record, deviations and inference limits.
- Findings and supporting objective observations.
- Conflict/impartiality clearance and assignment-specific competence authorization.
- Draft report, including scope, methodology, limitations, factual review and disposition.
- Prior review comments, corrections and revision history, if any.

The reviewer records missing inputs as an exception. Missing critical inputs prevent acceptance; the reviewer must not infer that an absent record exists.

## 6. Technical review method

The reviewer performs and records checks proportionate to scope and risk:

1. **Scope and criteria:** verify that conclusions remain within the agreed scope and use identified, current and applicable criteria.
2. **Traceability:** trace material report statements and each finding to requirement/source clauses and evidence IDs; verify that links resolve to the correct version.
3. **Evidence quality:** challenge authenticity, origin, completeness, currency, approval, relevance, sufficiency and contradictory evidence; distinguish received, examined and sufficient.
4. **Sampling:** verify population definition, selection rationale, sample coverage, exclusions, deviations and limitations; reject unsupported generalization.
5. **Finding quality:** verify objective wording, requirement reference, evidence basis, status, significance rationale and recommended action; separate fact from inference or opinion.
6. **Consistency:** compare matrix, findings, summary, conclusion and report narrative for contradictions, omissions, arithmetic errors and unsupported claims.
7. **Limitations and fairness:** verify exclusions, inaccessible records, unresolved uncertainty, client factual comments and reasoned dispositions are accurately represented.
8. **Claims and intended use:** verify the report does not imply certification, principal qualification, statutory compliance, tender eligibility, or approval beyond documented authority.
9. **Document integrity:** verify report ID, version, date, authorship, reviewer identity, controlled references, confidentiality marking and distribution controls.

The reviewer records a result and comment for every applicable checklist item. “Not applicable” requires a rationale. A review signature without recorded scope, checks and disposition is insufficient evidence.

## 7. Findings, corrections and re-review

- Each review comment receives a unique ID, severity, affected section/record, objective description, required action, owner, due date, status and closure evidence.
- The author/assessor responds to comments and makes corrections; the reviewer verifies the response and records whether it resolves the issue.
- The reviewer must not silently rewrite the assessment or accept an unsupported author response. Material changes to evidence interpretation or conclusions require explicit rationale and re-review.
- Preserve original versions and review comments. Do not overwrite or backdate issued records. Use controlled revision identifiers and record what changed, why, who changed it, who reviewed it, and which conclusions were affected.
- New evidence or a changed source/criterion triggers impact assessment and review of all affected findings and report sections.
- A minor correction may be verified by the reviewer without restarting the full review only when the reviewer records why it cannot affect conclusions. Major corrections require re-review of affected sections and linked conclusions.

## 8. Disagreement and escalation

1. The author may submit a reasoned response with supporting evidence; disagreement alone does not close a review comment.
2. The reviewer records the unresolved technical question, positions, evidence considered, potential impact and interim report restriction.
3. Escalate to a designated technical authority or independent senior reviewer who has relevant competence and no involvement in the disputed work.
4. If no competent independent resolution is available, maintain the report on hold. Do not resolve disagreement through commercial pressure or by deleting a substantiated finding without rationale.
5. Record the escalation decision, authority, rationale, conditions and any dissenting view. Material changes must be visible in the controlled report/review record.
6. Complaints or allegations of bias are routed through the separate impartiality/complaints process, not decided solely by the reviewer whose conduct is challenged.

## 9. Review outcome and report release

The reviewer records one of the defined outcomes and signs/dates the review record. “Accept subject to minor corrections” is conditional: corrections must be verified and logged before release.

The authorized report approver confirms, at minimum:
- Required technical review is complete and accepted.
- All major issues and release-blocking comments are closed.
- Required factual review has been completed or its status/limitations are transparently recorded.
- Report version matches the reviewed version; any subsequent material change was re-reviewed.
- Scope, limitations, confidentiality and distribution are correct.
- Engagement, impartiality, competence and contractual release gates remain valid.

The report approver records the release decision, report ID/version, recipients or distribution class, date and authority. Technical review acceptance alone does not authorize issue.

## 10. Records and retention

Retain the review assignment, reviewer competence/authorization reference, conflict clearance, review package index, completed checklist, comment/correction log, escalation record, reviewed report version, approval/release record and revision history under the approved document-control and retention rules. Restrict access to authorized personnel and protect client information. Do not invent retention periods; apply the formally approved schedule and any legal hold.

## 11. Monitoring and procedure review

Monitor review timeliness, repeat findings, correction recurrence, rework, disagreement frequency, report withdrawals and post-issue errors. Review this procedure after the first authorized pilot, material criteria/process changes, significant review failure, or scheduled management review. Record changes under document control.
