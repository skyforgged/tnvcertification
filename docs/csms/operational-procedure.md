# CSMS Readiness Review — Operational Procedure

**Status:** Internal draft — approval required before service delivery.

**Version:** 0.1  
**Process owner:** [To be assigned]  
**Approval authority:** [To be assigned]

## 1. Purpose

Define a controlled process for handling the proposed **CSMS Readiness Review & Documentation Support** service, from initial enquiry through report issue and record closure.

This procedure governs a scoped readiness/documentation review only. It does not authorize TNCVCertification to issue a CSMS certificate, principal qualification, vendor approval, official score, or any other decision reserved for a principal/client or scheme owner.

## 2. Scope

Applies to internal personnel involved in:
- Enquiry screening and contract review.
- Identification and verification of principal/client requirements.
- Competence and impartiality review.
- Planning and conducting document/evidence reviews.
- Reporting, technical review, approval, and record control.

It does not establish a certification scheme or replace any principal's own CSMS process.

## 4. Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Business development / enquiry owner | Capture enquiry accurately; do not promise approval, scores, certificates, or tender outcomes |
| Service owner | Confirm service scope, resources, and readiness to accept engagement |
| Assessor / reviewer | Conduct review against agreed criteria and retain traceable evidence |
| Technical reviewer | Independently review technical basis, findings, and report consistency |
| Impartiality / compliance reviewer | Evaluate conflicts, safeguards, claims, and any required authorization |
| Report approver | Authorize issue only after all required reviews are complete |
| Document controller | Control templates, records, access, retention, and revisions |

One person may hold multiple roles only where competence, independence, and impartiality are demonstrably protected. The same person should not advise a client on closing a gap and then independently make a conformity decision on that same work where this creates a conflict. If safeguards are not effective, decline or narrow the engagement.

## 5. Procedure

### 5.1 Enquiry intake and initial screening

Record:
- Legal identity and contact details of the contractor.
- Target principal/client and project, tender, or vendor category.
- Sector, work activities, and operating locations.
- Requested service (requirement mapping, document review, readiness review, workshop).
- Requested deliverables, deadlines, and any principal submission requirements.

Explain that the proposed service is not a principal approval, formal CSMS certificate, official score, or guarantee of tender acceptance.

**Reject or hold** enquiries that request fabricated, backdated, misleading, or falsely attributed evidence, or that require TNCVCertification to claim authority it does not hold.

### 5.2 Requirement-source identification and verification

Before accepting a readiness conclusion:
1. Obtain the applicable requirement document directly from the client/principal or another traceable authoritative source.
2. Record exact title, owner, revision/issue date, source, and date accessed.
3. Confirm the document applies to the contractor, work scope, project, and vendor category.
4. Identify related appendices, portal instructions, forms, updates, and clarifications.
5. Resolve conflicting or ambiguous requirements with the client/principal where possible.
6. Record limitations if the source is incomplete, uncontrolled, or cannot be verified.

Do not assume one principal's framework applies to another. Do not adopt a competitor's checklist as a universal standard. If no reliable applicable criteria can be established, offer only a clearly limited document-organization service or decline the readiness assessment.

### 5.3 Contract and scope review

Confirm in writing:
- Objectives, deliverables, criteria, boundaries, locations, and dates.
- Review methods (document review, interview, site observation, record sampling).
- Client responsibilities for completeness, authenticity, and timely access to records.
- Confidentiality, data handling, report distribution, and retention.
- Fees, schedule, change control, and acceptance criteria for TNCVCertification's deliverables.
- Explicit exclusions and limitations, including no guarantee of principal acceptance.

Changes to criteria, sites, work scope, or deliverables require documented review and client agreement.

### 5.4 Competence, impartiality, and authorization gate

Before assignment:
- Verify assessor competence against the subject matter and scope.
- Review prior advisory, consultancy, training, employment, financial, or other relationships that may affect impartiality.
- Define safeguards, independent review, or role separation.
- Verify any legal, scheme-owner, or principal authorization required for the activity and for any proposed claim.
- Record the decision to proceed, apply safeguards, restrict scope, or decline.

No formal certification, accreditation, official qualification, or principal authorization claim may be made without documentary evidence and approval.

### 5.5 Planning and evidence intake

The assessor prepares the assessment form and matrix with:
- Agreed criteria and clause references.
- Document request list and evidence IDs.
- Assessment schedule, locations, interview roles, and sampling plan.
- Access, safety, confidentiality, and data-protection arrangements.
- Known exclusions and risks to the review.

Log evidence received with title, owner/source, version/date, receipt date, and storage reference. Preserve original records where authorized. Do not create or alter client records on the client's behalf in a way that misrepresents authorship, approval, implementation, or historical timing.

### 5.6 Assessment and evidence review

For each requirement:
1. Identify the source clause and applicability.
2. Record evidence examined, including ID, version/date, and source.
3. Apply the approved status vocabulary: Evidence available; Partially evidenced; Evidence missing; Requires clarification; Not applicable (with rationale); or Not reviewed.
4. Describe objective observations and distinguish absence of evidence from evidence of non-implementation.
5. Record limitations, sample basis, and unresolved questions.
6. Discuss factual misunderstandings with the client and retain the disposition of corrections.

Sampling must be proportionate to the agreed scope and documented. Do not imply that unexamined sites, personnel, records, or activities were verified.

### 5.7 Findings and action priorities

Findings must be:
- Traceable to an identified criterion or explicitly labelled as an observation outside the criteria.
- Supported by identified evidence or a clear statement that evidence was unavailable.
- Factual, clear, and proportionate.
- Free of unsupported legal conclusions or guarantees.

Priorities may be described qualitatively with a documented rationale (for example, urgent, high, medium, low) only if the method is explained and approved. Do not present an internal priority as the principal's official score or rating.

Recommendations must not imply that TNCVCertification has implemented controls for the contractor. The contractor remains responsible for its own documents, approvals, implementation, and records.

### 5.8 Reporting and technical review

Prepare the report using the controlled report template. At minimum it must state:
- Client, principal, project, and review dates.
- Applicable criteria and their revisions.
- Scope, method, sampling, evidence reviewed, and exclusions.
- Findings, evidence status, and recommended actions.
- Conclusion limited to the reviewed evidence and agreed scope.
- Limitations and disclaimer that the report is not certification or principal approval.

A technical reviewer checks traceability, accuracy, consistency, evidence sufficiency, and language. Any unresolved disagreement is recorded and escalated before report approval.

### 5.9 Approval, issue, and post-issue changes

Only the designated report approver may authorize release. Confirm:
- Required reviews and conflict safeguards are complete.
- All findings and conclusion are supported.
- Client identity, criteria, dates, and version are correct.
- Disclaimer and distribution controls are present.

Issue the report as a controlled document with report number, version, date, and recipient list. Corrections after issue must be handled through a controlled revised version or formal addendum; retain the superseded version and reason for change.

### 5.10 Closeout and records

Retain, under the approved retention and access policy:
- Enquiry and contract review records.
- Requirement sources and verification notes.
- Competence and impartiality checks.
- Assessment plan, evidence register, matrix, and completed form.
- Findings, technical review, approval, issued report, and correspondence.
- Complaints, corrections, and post-issue changes.

Restrict access to authorized personnel. Apply applicable privacy, confidentiality, security, and retention requirements. Do not retain personal or sensitive information beyond the approved purpose and period.

## 6. Complaints, appeals, and disputes

A client may submit a complaint about service delivery or report accuracy through the published company channel. Record, acknowledge, investigate, and respond under the company's approved complaint process. The person investigating should be independent of the activity complained about where practicable.

Because this service does not make a formal certification or principal qualification decision, do not describe the process as an appeal against a certification decision. Direct disputes about principal scoring, qualification, or procurement outcomes to the relevant principal/client.

## 7. Service suspension or refusal conditions

Suspend, narrow, or decline work if:
- Applicable criteria cannot be identified or verified.
- Required competence or authorization is unavailable.
- An unmanaged conflict of interest exists.
- Evidence integrity is doubtful or falsification is requested.
- The client withholds material information or access.
- Scope changes make the original review unreliable.
- The client intends to misrepresent the report as a certificate, official score, or principal approval.

Record the reason, decision-maker, client communication, and any work already completed.

## 8. Performance monitoring and review

The service owner should periodically review:
- Enquiry-to-engagement conversion and reasons for refusal.
- Timeliness and report rework.
- Complaints, corrections, and client feedback.
- Findings traceability and technical review outcomes.
- Changes in principal requirements and source documents.
- Competence, impartiality, and authorization status.
- Whether public descriptions remain accurate and approved.

Review this procedure when requirements, service scope, legal conditions, or organizational controls change, and at a defined interval once the service is approved.
